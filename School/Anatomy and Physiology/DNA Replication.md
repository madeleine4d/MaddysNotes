- An initial protein is able to bind an [[Replication Origin]] along the dsDNA. This opens a small area of the double helix into two single strands.

- topoisomerase “unwinds” the alpha helix of the dsDNA allowing for further replication steps. It does this by breaking some of the bonds in the back bone temporarily.
- Helicase unwinds this opening to create a larger area of single stranded DNA by breaking [[H-bonds]].

![[rep forks.png]]

- Primers attach to the newly opened single stranded DNA. Primers are made by DNA primase enzyme out of RNA
- This primer is required for DNA polymerase to replicate the single strands
- Polymerase (pol) forms new DNA from 3’ end to 5’ end
- Polymerase III is responsible for making most of the new DNA. It forms DNA continuously from the 3’ end of a primer

![[Pasted image 20230808003742.png]]

- Because DNA Pol III can only add to one end, new primers are formed behind the main strand of DNA (leading strand). More DNA Pol III forms more DNA strands from these primers (lagging strand)

![[Pasted image 20230808003807.png]]

- RNase H removed all but the last base of the primers. DNA Pol I fills in the gap and removed the last RNA base
- ligase then connects the backbone to make continuous DNA

## speed of replication
- E. Coli polymerase travels at as fast as 700 base pairs per second.
- polymerase makes 1 mistake every $10^7$ nucleotides and after error correction that is more like 1 out of every $10^9$.
## Repair and proofreading
- DNA Pol III is also able to proofread DNA
- exonuclease activity of DNA Pol III: when DNA Pol III detects that the wrong base is added it is able to move backward (3’-5’) and removed that base, replacing it with the correct one, but only from the end of the strand. 
- exonuclease activity of DNA Pol I: the same as for DNA Pol III but occurs in the 5’-3’ direction.
- endonuclease activity: removal of a base from the middle of a strand.
### mismatch repair

1. if a mistake is not caught by DNA Pol III, a protein recognizes the distortion of the sugar backbone caused by the mismatch. This occurs after replication.
2. The area is marked with a cut.
3. an exonuclease removes that incorrect nucleotide.
4. DNA Pol replaces the missing nucleotide.
5. DNA ligase re-ligates the sugar backbone.

we do not know how this works in eukaryotes. In prokaryotes this occurs via detection of mentholated adenines. 

### DNA damage
DNA damage is distinct from mutations as it is a change to the structure of the DNA and not to its sequence.

##### Exogenous:
coming form without the organism, such as short wavelength EM radiation. UV radiation causes pyrimidine dimerization between adjacent bases. Resulting in T-T and C-C base pairs. This causes a protrusion of the sugar backbone.
##### Endogenous:
coming from within the organism, such as reactive oxygen species. Superoxides, peroxites, etc. A byproduct of [[electron transport chain]].   
##### repair:
1. endonuclease removes the dimerized bases.
2. DNA Pol replaces them.
3. DNA ligase ligates the sugar backbone.

### failure of repair
#### for mutation
when the above occurs and is not repaired it results in a point mutation in one of the later replicated dsDNA strands.
